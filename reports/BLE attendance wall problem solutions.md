# Make the wall block the proof

**Tuning RSSI on one ESP32 beacon will not reliably separate a phone inside the staff room from one held against the outside of its wall.** A typical brick or plaster partition removes only about **2–12 dB** at 2.4 GHz. Handset model, grip and body shadowing move a cheap phone's reading by **6–20 dB**. Hospitals, Microsoft Teams Rooms and classroom-attendance vendors all reached the same fix: keep Bluetooth for coarse discovery and add a second signal that walls physically stop. For staff carrying ordinary Android phones, that signal is **near-ultrasound**, because every phone has a microphone. A **rotating QR code on an in-room display** is the fallback that works on every phone. The recommended build has four parts. It adds roughly **₹350–650 of audio parts** to the existing ESP32 so it plays an 18–19.5 kHz rotating HMAC code, which the Flutter app records for three seconds during the face scan. It turns the BLE check into a windowed *difference* between the inside beacon and a guard beacon placed at the doorway, which is where sound leaks. It shows a rotating QR code inside the room for phones that cannot hear the tone. And it keeps the existing face liveness, mock-GPS rejection and short server-side freshness windows. Together these defeat the threat you named, a colleague standing just outside the wall, and they block replay. No low-cost design stops an insider who live-forwards codes to an absent colleague; face liveness, random re-checks and a "reverse" acoustic challenge make that harder but cannot prevent it. Channel Sounding, UWB, BLE direction finding and Wi-Fi RTT each fail at least one of three tests: they need flagship phones, they need non-ESP32 chips, or they measure distance rather than which side of a wall the phone is on. They belong on a later roadmap, not in a hackathon build.

## A 2–12 dB wall drowns in 6–20 dB of phone noise

At 2.4 GHz the wall is a weak filter. A 2025 design note puts plasterboard at **2–4 dB**, single clear glass at **2–3 dB**, 90–110 mm brick at **8–12 dB**, and 150 mm concrete at **12–20 dB** ([zencontrol](https://support.zencontrol.com/hc/en-us/articles/13691317355919-Wireless-Thread-Signal-Strength-Path-Loss-Practical-Design-Note)). The CWNA study guide uses about 12 dB for brick and block ([Aruba/CWNA](https://community.arubanetworks.com/viewthread?MID=17983)), while iBwave lists lime brick at only 4.3 dB ([iBwave](https://blog.ibwave.com/?p=3095)). That disagreement is itself a warning: you cannot look up your wall's loss, you have to measure it. Doors, windows and ventilators in the same wall leak through 2–3 dB gaps, so the worst case is set by the openings, not the brick.

The phone side is noisier than the wall. Trinity College Dublin's measurements found that handset model, orientation and absorption by bodies and bags shift BLE RSSI by an amount **"comparable with that caused by moving 2 m"**, and that RSSI "need not decrease with increasing distance" ([Leith & Farrell](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.06822v1)). Different phones read up to **6 dB apart** at the same spot ([phone comparison study](https://www.researchgate.net/figure/Comparison-of-BLE-RSSI-values-for-different-phones-at-the-same-position-and-orientation_fig1_317423547)). On Pixel phones the three advertising channels differed by up to **15 dB** at one distance ([Gentner et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.09099)). Smartphone measurement papers report **10–20 dB** between line-of-sight and body-blocked readings ([arXiv 2006.15711](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.15711); [arXiv 2006.09099](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.09099)). Google and Apple's Exposure Notification system had to keep a per-model calibration table of transmit and receive offsets for this reason ([BeaconZone](https://beaconzone.co.uk/blog/?p=5616)).

A log-distance model makes the consequence concrete. These are illustrative calculations from the research notes, not measurements. Take an 8 × 6 m room with the beacon on the ceiling at the centre, transmitting at +9 dBm. The **worst inside position beats the best position 0.3 m outside a common wall by only −1 to +9 dB**. A beacon on a desk against the wall is worse: someone 0.8 m outside that wall reads stronger than an honest colleague 7.5 m away inside. The two people also behave differently. A proxy outside the wall holds the phone up and finds the best angle. An honest staff member keeps the phone in a pocket or bag. Any threshold loose enough to accept the pocket will accept the motivated outsider.

Field evidence agrees. In a WPI in-room presence study, a −60 dBm threshold **worked only when the beacon was mounted outside the entrance door** and did not carry over to other placements ([WPI CWINS](https://www.cwins.wpi.edu/publications/conference%20paper/Sushi.pdf)). Peer-reviewed BLE room classification reaches **70–90%** with a few beacons ([Sensors 2021](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8197460/)), and a hospital-room study managed **81%** on a binary in/out decision ([PMC9904895](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9904895/)). Kontakt.io's own figures rate BLE-only tags at **90%+** room accuracy with a gateway in the room and **75%+** without one ([Kontakt.io](https://support.kontakt.io/hc/en-gb/articles/14512613170972-Intro-to-Beam-Mini-with-Cisco-Spaces)). Midmark launched a BLE network in February 2026 at "3–5 meter accuracy" and markets it as a tier below its infrared product ([Midmark](https://www.midmark.com/newsroom/news-releases/2026/02/17/midmark-rtls-launches-ble-sensory-network-for-smarter--scalable-rtls)).

The current codebase sits at the most permissive end of this range. The firmware advertises at **+9 dBm**, the ESP32 maximum (`firmware/staff_beacon/config.h`). The app keeps only the **latest advertisement** per beacon (`mobile/lib/features/beacon/beacon_controller.dart`). The server's default threshold is **−85 dBm** (`backend/app/services/attendance_service.py`). In the same model, a phone 3 m behind a 10 dB brick wall from a +9 dBm beacon still reads about −53 dBm, roughly 30 dB above that threshold.

Lowering transmit power does not fix this. The ESP32 supports −12 to +9 dBm ([Espressif](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-techpedia/en/latest/esp-friends/advanced-development/performance/modify-tx-power.html)), but every dB removed lowers inside and outside readings equally. It shortens the range at which the beacon can be heard, but the margin between inside and just-outside stays the same. Forum users also report that `esp_ble_tx_power_set` sometimes has no visible effect, so check any change with a phone ([esp32.com](https://esp32.com/viewtopic.php?p=29260)). Placement does help. A ceiling-centre beacon at least 3 m from any wall that faces an accessible area makes the outside path longer and more oblique through the wall. The one RSSI-only design that changes the picture is a **tap zone**. Set the beacon to −12 dBm and require the phone within 10–30 cm of it. The model margin then grows to about **23–32 dB**, larger than any handset offset, because the decision now rests on a large distance ratio rather than on the wall. The cost is that staff must walk to a check-in point.

## A corridor guard beacon roughly triples the RF margin, but only against unequipped proxies

The cheapest RF upgrade is to stop asking "is the beacon loud?" and ask "is the inside beacon louder than the corridor beacon?" Put a second, authenticated ESP32 **1–2 m beyond the wall in the corridor**, at the same transmit power. Accept a check-in only if the windowed median of *inside minus guard* exceeds a calibrated margin. In the notes' model (10 dB wall, room centre beacon, guard 1.5 m outside), the worst inside position scores about **+7.6 dB** and the best outside position about **−12.8 dB**. That is roughly **20 dB of separation, against about 6 dB for a single beacon**. Two effects produce the gain. The wall's loss now counts in the right direction from both sides. And each phone's constant receiver offset cancels, because the same radio measures both beacons. The indoor-positioning literature reached the same conclusion: signal-strength-difference maps reduce device-dependent bias ([Yang et al.](https://doaj.org/article/11a87ca35dcd4a189754bc8575e89206)), rank-based features hold up better across devices than raw values ([arXiv 2106.02777](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.02777)), and vendors advise comparing the strongest receiver with the second-best rather than trusting an absolute level ([Lansitec](https://www.lansitec.com/blogs/ble-presence-detection/)). Body shadowing does not cancel, because it depends on direction. A temporal window is still needed, and the 20 dB figure is a model result that the team must confirm by measurement.

Placement decides whether the guard works. A guard placed *inside* the room near the wall fails, because honest staff near that wall hear it strongly too. Guards belong on the far side of each wall that faces a corridor, verandah or open ground. A wall shared with another staff-only room needs none. The guard must also carry a rotating token. If it advertises a static ID, an attacker can spoof a weak guard and tilt the difference in their favour. A simpler "require all inside beacons above threshold" layout is weaker, because a person outside the middle of a long wall still hears both beacons, and the absolute thresholds keep the phone-to-phone offsets.

Time-windowing is cheap and mainly fixes *false rejections*. Take the median over 10–20 s rather than the latest packet, and require the condition on at least 80% of samples. This absorbs fading and momentary body blocking for honest users, but a patient proxy standing still outside the wall is stable too. Android allows an app to start or stop BLE scans at most **5 times per 30 s**; past that, scans silently return nothing for about 30 s ([Punch Through](https://punchthrough.com/ble-scan-returns-no-results-on-android/)). So run one continuous low-latency scan and buffer readings. Do not restart the scan to sample.

Machine learning on RSSI adds little here. Random forests hit **99.95%** on a small controlled grid ([Sensors 2021](https://doi.org/10.3390/s21134605)) but about **91%** in a 30-beacon whole-floor deployment ([deployment study](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/367762231_Fingerprint-based_indoor_positioning_system_using_BLE_real_deployment_study)). Changing only the receiver hardware moved one study's accuracy by about 10 points ([Sensors 2021](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8197460/)), which is the cheap-Android problem in miniature. Wi-Fi fingerprinting reached **94.74%** on in/out classification in a 2026 BUET seminar ([BUET CSE](https://cse.buet.ac.bd/home/news_detail/241)), and a 2025 ACM paper documents Wi-Fi attendance errors in both directions ([ACM](https://unpaywall.org/10.1145%2F3704522.3704523)). With two to four beacons and a yes/no label, a windowed differential rule is effectively a one-feature classifier that keeps most of the possible gain, with no site survey to maintain.

All RF geometry shares one ceiling: it stops the *unequipped* proxy, not the equipped one. Kim, Lee and Paek cloned a university's commercial beacon and marked absent students present. They then defeated their own proposed countermeasures ([IEEE Access 2018](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/8555557/)). The rotating HMAC token already blocks that cloning, but it does nothing about the wall. A token received through brick is still valid. Researchers have relayed BLE advertisements between locations over the internet using basic tools such as a phone or a Raspberry Pi ([Baumgärtner et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.05914)). Recorded beacon messages are indistinguishable from genuine ones within their validity window ([arXiv 2107.05868](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.05868)). Relay tooling adds as little as **about 8 ms** of round-trip latency ([NCC Group](https://www.nccgroup.com/research-blog/technical-advisory-ble-proximity-authentication-vulnerable-to-relay-attacks/)). Analog physical-layer relays have extended Bluetooth links beyond **90 m** ([CASA Bochum](https://casa.rub.de/fileadmin/img/Publikationen_PDFs/2022_Analog_Physical-Layer_Relay_Attacks_with_Application_to_Bluetooth_and_Phase_Based_Ranging_Publication_ClusterofExcellence_CASA_Bochum.pdf)). An ESP32 with an external antenna pressed to the outside wall, re-broadcasting at full power next to the attacker's phone, controls the received RSSI and beats any RSSI rule.

## Precision ranging exists, but not on a ₹10,000 phone

**BLE direction finding (AoA/AoD) is unavailable to apps.** Nordic states plainly that "smartphones do not support Bluetooth Direction Finding" ([Nordic](https://www.nordicsemi.com/Products/Wireless/Bluetooth-Direction-Finding)). No Espressif chip in the team's reach exposes it: an ESP-IDF issue found no CTE implementation for the ESP32-C6 ([esp-idf #13531](https://github.com/espressif/esp-idf/issues/13531)), and only the ESP32-H4, sampling since March 2026, lists AoA/AoD ([Espressif](https://www.espressif.com/en/news/ESP32_H4_Sample)). Antenna-array kits cost **USD 149–299** ([Silicon Labs](https://silabs.com/development-tools/wireless/bluetooth/bg22-rb4191a-bg22-bluetooth-dual-polarized-antenna-array-radio-board)). Even a working array would report an angle, not which side of a wall the phone is on.

**Bluetooth Channel Sounding** is the first secure, roughly sub-metre Bluetooth ranging method, and Android 16 exposes it through `RangingManager` ([Android Developers](https://developer.android.com/develop/connectivity/ranging)). But the Pixel 10 (August 2025) was the first phone with the hardware ([Nordic DevZone](https://devzone.nordicsemi.com/nordic/nordic-blog/b/blog/posts/evaluating-bluetooth-channel-sounding-with-our-open_2d00_source-android-app-on-google-pixel-10)). ESP-IDF lists Channel Sounding as unsupported on the ESP32-S3 ([ESP-IDF](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-idf/en/v6.0/esp32s3/api-guides/ble/ble-feature-support-status.html)). One developer's demo "hovered around 1 m" rather than the advertised precision ([Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45913870)). It needs both a flagship phone and a Nordic, Silicon Labs or NXP beacon, so it can only be an optional "verified" tier.

**UWB** is the one RF technology here that could follow a wall. It ranges to about **10 cm**, and provisioned STS keys on Android 14+ resist replay and relay ([Android UWB](https://developer.android.com/develop/connectivity/uwb)). Ranging through a concrete wall or a human body still erred by only about 44–60 cm ([arXiv 2104.11042](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.11042)). Android support, however, covers Pixel Pro and Pixel 8+, Galaxy S/Note/Fold, and the Moto Edge 50 Ultra ([Android UWB](https://developer.android.com/develop/connectivity/uwb)). Premium phones above ₹30,000 were only **22%** of Indian shipments in 2025 ([GSMArena/Counterpoint](https://www.gsmarena.com/counterpoint_1_in_5_smartphones_shipped_in_india_in_2025_were_premium_models-news-71366.php)). Qorvo anchor kits cost **US$29.50** each ([RFMW](https://rfmw.com/products/detail/dwm3001cdk-qorvo/692466/)), and the leading Flutter plugin lists no Android-to-accessory pairing ([flutter_uwb](https://pub.dev/packages/flutter_uwb)).

**Wi-Fi RTT** measures a radius, not a boundary. With three or more good access points, phones reach **1–2 m** ([Android Wi-Fi RTT](https://developer.android.com/develop/connectivity/wifi/wifi-rtt)). An ESP32 used as the responder fares worse. Espressif itself warned that phone-to-ESP32 accuracy "will be very low" ([esp-idf #6810](https://github.com/espressif/esp-idf/issues/6810)), and an ESP32-S2 benchmark showed **about 1 m of error at 0.5 m and 2–3 m at 1–1.5 m** ([arXiv 2303.03766](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.03766v1)). Google's support list includes some Galaxy A and older Redmi Note models, but support depends on the manufacturer ([Android Wi-Fi RTT](https://developer.android.com/develop/connectivity/wifi/wifi-rtt)). No Indian market share figure for RTT-capable phones exists.

**Requiring a connection to the staff-room Wi-Fi network** (a BSSID or SSID check) has the same wall problem as BLE: a 10 cm concrete wall cost only **8.9 dB** in one measurement ([IEICE](https://ken.ieice.org/ken/paper/20091217ZarQ/eng/)). Anyone can also set up an access point with the right SSID ([US 9883479](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/9883479)).

## Hospitals buy certainty with signals walls stop, and phones can receive three of them

Clinical location vendors stopped trying to make RF respect walls. Kontakt.io's table is the clearest evidence. BLE-only tags reach **90%+** room accuracy with an in-room gateway, while BLE plus infrared tags reach a claimed **99.99%** ([Kontakt.io](https://support.kontakt.io/hc/en-gb/articles/14512613170972-Intro-to-Beam-Mini-with-Cisco-Spaces)). CenTrak sells Gen2IR on the basis that it "does not pass through walls" ([CenTrak](https://tours.centrak.com/answers/multi-mode-technology-gen2ir-and-wi-fi-rtls-for-healthcare)). AeroScout's ultrasound exciter "does not pass through walls, enabling room-level detection" ([CDW](https://www.cdw.com/product/aeroscout-ex4330-ultrasound-exciter/5232297)). Microsoft's Teams Rooms Proximity Join favours ultrasound because it "typically doesn't pass through walls," while Bluetooth "can penetrate through walls" ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoftteams/rooms/proximity-join)). CourseKey takes classroom attendance with an inaudible tone carrying a unique code ([Campus Technology](https://campustechnology.com/Articles/2017/10/02/New-CourseKey-Feature-Takes-Attendance-with-Sound.aspx)). These certainty figures are vendor-stated and none has been independently validated. Still, the industry's architecture is consistent: RF tells you roughly where; a wall-bounded carrier tells you which room. Infrared depends on a dedicated receiver in the badge ([Kontakt.io Smart Badge 3](https://support.kontakt.io/hc/en-gb/articles/19676391233948-About-the-Smart-Badge-3)), and the research found no evidence of usable IR receivers in phones. For staff phones, the realistic wall-bounded carriers are therefore **sound (microphone), light (camera) and near-field magnetic coupling (NFC)**.

### Near-ultrasound: room-wide and passive, but it leaks through open doors

Sound insulation rises about **6 dB per doubling of frequency** under the mass law ([Ballagh](https://www.acoustics.org.nz/sites/www.acoustics.org.nz/files/journal/pdfs/Ballagh,_K_NZA2003.pdf)), so a masonry wall blocks 18 kHz much more strongly than it blocks 2.4 GHz radio. The weak points are the openings. Edge gaps of 1/8 inch cut a door rated STC-52 down to STC-21, "especially at medium to high frequencies" ([Trustile](https://www.trustile.com/technical-information/understanding-stc-ratings)). Cisco's help states that Proximity "doesn't work outside the meeting room when doors to the room are closed" ([Webex Help](https://help.webex.com/en-us/article/b5c5rab/Install-and-Use-the-Cisco-Proximity-App)), and its legal text concedes that through an open door a client "may be able to detect" the signal ([Cisco](https://www3-realm.cisco.com/web/siteassets/legal/proximity-supplement.html)). No measured wall or door transmission-loss data exists at 16–20 kHz, because lab standards stop near 5 kHz. The team will produce the first numbers for its own room.

Phones generally hear the band, but it is not guaranteed. Google's protocol uses 18.5–20 kHz on commodity phones and is "reliable at 2 m" and "often works at 10 m" indoors ([Getreuer et al.](https://www.academia.edu/164596957/Ultrasonic_Communication_Using_Consumer_Hardware)). Zoom's vendor spec requires **98% success at 5 m** ([Zoom](https://gozdm-developer.zoom.us/docs/VendorOsZRC/SupportUltrasonicProximity.html)). Android's compatibility definition only **"strongly recommends"** near-ultrasound recording support ([CDD 7.8](https://os.unplugged.com/werunplugged/unplugged-vendor/src/branch/main/compatibility/cdd/7_hardware-compatibility/7_8_audio.md)). It does require the `VOICE_RECOGNITION` source to capture at 44.1/48 kHz "with no band-pass or antialiasing filters" ([AOSP](https://source.android.com/docs/core/audio/implement-pre-processing)). Measured Android links show the usable band at about **18–20 kHz "with some variation among devices"** ([Illinois Tech](https://ecasp.ece.iit.edu/publications/2012-present/2020-10.pdf)). Some drivers still filter the "unprocessed" source ([Fairphone forum](https://forum.fairphone.com/t/very-low-quality-audio-recordings/54392?page=2)). A 2026 re-implementation study ran over **11,000** smartphone transmissions and found that distance, device model, handling and noise cause failures that simulations miss ([Putz et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.02249v1)). Plan for a minority of phones that cannot hear the tone, and detect them at enrolment.

The hardware and software exist and are cheap. A MAX98357A I2S amplifier accepts 8–96 kHz sample rates ([DFRobot](https://wiki.dfrobot.com/dfr0954)). It sells on Amazon.in bundled with an INMP441 mic for **₹299–499** ([price-history.in](https://price-history.in/product/max98357a-i2s-3w-class-d-amplifier-8KrHoqG5)). Piezo tweeters cost **₹30–95** ([IndiaMART](https://m.indiamart.com/impcat/piezo-speaker.html); [Sunil Electronics](https://m.indiamart.com/sunilelectronics-delhi/buzzer.html)). On the phone, the Flutter `record` 7.1.2 package streams PCM from `AndroidAudioSource.unprocessed` or `voiceRecognition`, with gain control, echo cancellation and noise suppression off by default ([pub.dev record](https://pub.dev/packages/record)). The pure-Dart `chirp_codec` decodes 4-FSK at 18–19.2 kHz with Goertzel filters ([pub.dev chirp_codec](https://pub.dev/packages/chirp_codec)). The MIT-licensed ggwave works too, but its stock "ultrasound" protocols start at **15 kHz** ([ggwave.h](https://github.com/ggerganov/ggwave/blob/master/include/ggwave/ggwave.h)), which many people can hear. About **5% of 5–19-year-olds** are 60 dB more sensitive at 20 kHz than the median 30–39-year-old ([Leighton](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/444416/1/Public_Exposure_to_Airborne_Ultrasound_and_Very_High_Frequency_Sound_Timothy_G._Leighton.pdf)), and LISNR's 18.7–19.2 kHz tones are claimed inaudible to 49 of 50 people ([Digital Trends](https://www.digitaltrends.com/cool-tech/lisnr-transfer-files-audio/)). So stay within **18.0–19.5 kHz** at the lowest level that covers the room. Microphone access also carries a privacy history. The FTC warned 12 developers whose apps listened for TV audio beacons without disclosure ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/press-releases/2016/03/ftc-issues-warning-letters-app-developers-using-silverpush-code)). Listen only in the foreground, only during check-in, decode on the device, and never upload audio.

### Rotating QR and NFC: hard physical limits at a fixed point

A QR code shown on a small display inside the room is a **line-of-sight proof that works on every camera phone**. Commercial systems refresh codes every 1–5 s so that screenshots are useless ([Pathway](https://pathwaylabs.notion.site/QR-Code-Attendance-Class-Level-198932b1ac788088952fc29c243030a4); [Diobox](https://help.d.io/en/articles/11638920-temporal-qr-codes)). One academic deployment logged **7,900 scans over 7 weeks** with 5-second codes ([Maratto](https://www.maratto.africa/research/design-and-implementation-of-an-enhanced-qr-code-d783ffbc56)). Its weakness is a live video relay from an accomplice within the refresh window. PayPal has patented splitting a code across display frames so that one photo cannot capture it ([US 11687910](https://patents.google.com/patent/US11687910)).

NFC is stricter still: at a few centimetres of range, a wall settles the question. Android has supported host card emulation since 4.4, and HCE devices use **random UIDs on each tap**, so authentication must run over a challenge rather than the UID ([Android HCE](https://developer.android.com/develop/connectivity/nfc/hce)). An NXP NTAG 424 DNA tag produces a fresh cryptographic MAC on every read ([NXP](https://www.nxp.com/products/rfid-nfc/nfc-hf/NTAG424DNA)) and costs about **US$0.35** in volume ([element14](https://tw.element14.com/en-TW/nxp/nt4h2421g0da8-02j/nfc-rfid-tags-transponders-nfc/dp/4784260)). Relaying it with NFCGate needs **two rooted phones** and an accomplice at the tag ([TU Darmstadt](https://tuprints.ulb.tu-darmstadt.de/5414)). Coverage is the catch. Samsung's Galaxy M16 brought tap-to-pay to its budget line only in 2025, and the M06 has no NFC ([SamMobile](https://www.sammobile.com/?p=16599540)). No reliable figure exists for the share of Indian phones with NFC.

Two other camera and sensor ideas fall short. An LED blinking a code to the camera is wall-proof and reaches **>1,200 b/s at up to 3 m** in lab work ([CPS-VO](https://archive.cps-vo.org/node/38847)), but no Flutter decoder exists and decoding depends on each phone's camera exposure handling. Barometer and magnetic sensors identify floors reliably, with **near-100%** floor-change detection ([Microsoft Research](https://microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2016/02/hotmobile14b.pdf)) and **0.5 m** altitude error ([PMC5855158](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5855158/)). But the best fused smartphone systems still have **2.8 m** third-quartile horizontal error ([IPIN 2023](https://evaal.isti.cnr.it/2023/results)), and the app reports every reading itself, so a modified app can fake them.

| UWB | 10 cm two-way ranging, multilateration | Strong with 3+ anchors | Flagships only | US$29.50–59.80 per anchor × 3–4 | Very high | High (STS) | Future flagship tier |
| **Near-ultrasound rotating code** | 18–19.5 kHz code; walls block, phone mic hears | Strong with door closed; leaks via open doors | Most phones; tail of failures (CDD only recommends support) | ₹350–650 | Medium | Medium–high (stops outsiders and replay; insider relay possible) | **Adopt as wall-bounded factor** |
| **Rotating QR on in-room display** | Line of sight to a code that changes every 2–5 s | Strong if hidden from door and windows | About 100% (camera) | ESP32 + small display | Low | Medium (live video relay possible) | **Adopt as universal fallback** |
| LED-to-camera optical code | Rolling-shutter decode of LED blinks | Strong (line of sight) | Varies with camera | LED | High, no Flutter library | Medium–high (untested) | Research only |
| NFC tap (NTAG 424 DNA or ESP32 + PN532 with HCE) | Centimetre-range coupling | Strongest | Many 2025+ budget phones; older ones lack it | Under ₹100 tag, or a reader module | Low–medium | Medium–high (relay needs root and an accomplice) | Optional fast path |
| Infrared | Light that walls stop | Strongest in hospitals | No phone receivers found | n/a | n/a | n/a | Not feasible on phones |
| Magnetic / barometer | Field or pressure fingerprint | Floor-level only | Barometer often missing (unverified) | Reference sensor | Medium | Low (app reports the reading) | Weak supporting signal |

Read across the table and one pattern stands out. **Every option that separates the two sides of a wall, runs on cheap phones and still holds up against a BLE re-broadcaster swaps the radio wave for a different physical carrier.** Every option that keeps radio but adds precision fails on phone support, on the type of measurement, or both. The choice for this team is therefore which wall-bounded carrier to lead with. The answer depends on user experience. Ultrasound is the only one that is passive and covers the whole room: it can run during the face scan without an extra step. QR and NFC demand a deliberate action at a fixed point, and in exchange offer near-deterministic decoding.

## Recommended build: sound for the walls, a guard beacon for the doorway

The two strongest cheap layers fail in opposite places. **BLE leaks through solid walls but can be held back at the doorway by a guard beacon. Near-ultrasound stops at solid walls but leaks through the open door.** Put guard beacons exactly where sound escapes (the corridor outside the door, and outside any window or ventilator facing an accessible area), and rely on sound where radio escapes (the solid walls). Then each layer covers the other's weak spot. A colleague behind a solid wall fails the acoustic check. A colleague in the doorway or corridor fails the inside-minus-guard check. Someone who cannot hear the tone falls back to the in-room QR code. The existing face liveness, face-replay detection (`face_probes`) and mock-GPS rejection (`backend/app/services/geofence.py`) continue to handle identity and remote marking. This follows the clinical RTLS architecture at hackathon cost, and it is easy to demo: a phone on the far side of the wall receives the BLE token but never the tone, and is rejected.

### Firmware outline

On the inside ESP32, keep protocol V3 BLE advertising and the existing NTP-synced HMAC generator, and add an I2S output on three GPIOs to the MAX98357A. Mount the tweeter high on the wall **opposite the door**, aimed into the room. Every 5 s, compute `audio_code = HMAC-SHA256(AUDIO_SECRET, beacon_id + ":A:" + floor(t/5))[0..3]`. Use a key separate from the BLE token so that leaking one does not expose the other. Frame it as preamble + 4-bit room ID + 32-bit code + CRC-8 (44 bits). Modulate it as **4-FSK at 18.00 / 18.25 / 18.50 / 18.75 kHz**, with 50 ms symbols and 5 ms raised-cosine ramps to avoid audible clicks. Stream it continuously from a 48 kHz sine table over I2S DMA. One frame takes about 1–1.5 s, so a 3 s capture holds at least two repetitions for majority voting. Alternatively, define a ggwave custom protocol with `freqStart` at bin 384 (18.0 kHz) and `bytesPerTx = 1` to stay within 18.0–19.5 kHz ([ggwave](https://github.com/ggerganov/ggwave)). Set the volume to the lowest level that decodes in the far corner, measured with a phone spectrogram app, and limit emission to attendance hours. Guard beacons run the same firmware with the audio path disabled. A QR display unit renders `HMAC(QR_SECRET, beacon_id + ":Q:" + floor(t/3))` every 3 s.

### Flutter outline

Keep `flutter_blue_plus` scanning continuously, without restarts. For each beacon ID, append (RSSI, timestamp) to a 15 s ring buffer, and submit the median, the 20th percentile and the sample count instead of the single latest value. A guard that is not heard counts as a floor value such as −100 dBm. When the face-capture screen opens, start a 3 s microphone stream at the same time:

```dart
recorder.startStream(const RecordConfig(
  encoder: AudioEncoder.pcm16bits, sampleRate: 48000, numChannels: 1,
  autoGain: false, echoCancel: false, noiseSuppress: false,
  androidConfig: AndroidRecordConfig(audioSource: AndroidAudioSource.unprocessed)));
// if nothing above 17 kHz on this model: retry with AndroidAudioSource.voiceRecognition
```

Band-pass at 17.5–20 kHz, find the preamble, run one Goertzel filter per tone per symbol, check the CRC, and compute the in-band SNR. Send only `{audio_code, snr, window}` with the BLE readings and the face result; never the audio. At device binding, run a **capability check** next to the beacon. If the phone cannot reach the room's SNR threshold at 18.5 kHz, mark it `audio_incapable` and route it to the QR path: scan the rotating QR with the rear camera, then complete the face capture within about 10 s. Ask for `RECORD_AUDIO` at first check-in with a plain explanation that the app listens for 3 seconds for an inaudible signal from the staff-room beacon and that audio never leaves the phone.

### Server rule

The room proof should be a **hard gate**, not one more weight in `confidence.py`. Today a faint beacon only lowers a 20-point signal component.

```python
# pseudo-code for a new room check in attendance_service.py
audio_ok = (hmac_valid(audio_code, AUDIO_SECRET, window_s=5, skew=1)
            and snr >= room.audio_snr_min
            and audio_beacon_id == ble_beacon_id)
qr_ok = (hmac_valid(qr_code, QR_SECRET, window_s=3, skew=1)
         and face_started_at - qr_scanned_at <= 10)          # seconds
ble_ok = (inside_median - guard_median >= room.diff_margin_db
          and inside_samples >= MIN_SAMPLES)
accept = (token_ok and ble_ok and (audio_ok or qr_ok)
          and face_live and not mocked_gps
          and first_use(staff_id, room_code, window))
```

Calibrate `diff_margin_db` from a walk test rather than guessing. In the model, a threshold near 0 dB sits about 10 dB from both the worst inside and the best outside reading. Log the device model with every decision so you can see which handsets fail.

| Attacker | BLE differential | Ultrasound | QR fallback | Face liveness + replay check | Result |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Colleague just outside a solid wall, door closed | Partial (depends on guard coverage) | **Blocks** | **Blocks** (no line of sight) | n/a (genuine face) | Blocked |
| Colleague in corridor at an open door | **Blocks** (guard dominates) | May leak | Blocks if display hidden from door | n/a | Blocked |
| Outsider re-broadcasting BLE at high power | Defeated | **Blocks** | Blocks | n/a | Blocked unless sound is also relayed |
| Replay of recorded code or token later | Token expires | **Blocks** (5 s window, single use) | Blocks (3 s window) | n/a | Blocked |
| Insider live-forwards decoded code to an outsider's modified app | Defeated if also relayed | Defeated | Defeated by live video | n/a (outsider's face is real) | **Harder, not blocked**: reverse challenge and random re-checks raise the cost |
| Insider holds absent colleague's phone | Passes | Passes | Passes | **Blocks** | Blocked |
| Photo or video reuse, fake GPS from home (Bihar pattern) | Blocks | Blocks | Blocks | **Blocks** | Blocked |

That last row reflects documented Indian experience. Bihar's photo-plus-GPS teacher app accepted check-ins within **500 m** ([Scroll.in](https://scroll.in/article/1075292/bihar-is-using-an-app-to-track-teachers-attendance-much-to-their-anger)). Teachers defeated it by reusing photos for up to 10 days, using other people's phones, uploading old videos and stripping GPS ([Patna Press](https://patnapress.com/bihar-teacher-attendance-scam-photos-fake-locations-and-same-clothes-for-10-days-expose-e-shiksha-fraud/)). The NMC's own faculty geofence is **100 m** ([The South First](https://thesouthfirst.com/news/fingerprint-biometric-system-fails-in-medical-colleges-nmc-switches-to-face-based-attendance-for-faculty)), and GPS indoors is "easily 30–50 m off" ([Offision](https://offision.com/help/en/attendance/verify-clock-ins-with-gps-ip-and-selfies/)). GPS therefore proves campus, never room. The insider-relay row is the honest limit. Real relay resistance needs distance bounding, which commodity Android BLE does not provide. Two partial answers fit this budget. The first is the reverse acoustic challenge: the phone plays a fresh server nonce and the in-room ESP32 must hear it, which forces a relay to place a live speaker inside the room. Car-entry patents use the same principle, because relaying sound distorts timing ([US 10953851](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/10953851)). The second is short, unpredictable verification windows of the kind Acadly uses (2.5 minutes) ([Acadly](https://pages.acadly.com/rfums-for-students)).

### Calibration that produces your own evidence

No published study reports budget-Android performance for this combination in an Indian staff room, so the team's walk test becomes its evidence. Use at least five phone models (Redmi, Realme, vivo, Samsung Galaxy A/M). Collect 30 s at grid points inside the room, at the threshold, in the corridor with the door open and with it closed, directly behind each wall, and in the adjacent room. Log the inside-minus-guard median, the audio decode result and SNR, and QR success. Report **inside acceptance %** and **outside false-accept %** per layer and combined. Also measure your actual wall: walk both sides at two TX powers. The published loss figures for brick range from 4.3 to 12 dB.

## Conclusion

The wall problem is a physics problem, not a tuning problem. At 2.4 GHz the boundary you care about is smaller than the noise floor of the receivers you must support. Calibration, ML and premium ranging all try to measure a smaller and smaller difference with the same carrier. What works is to change the carrier, or at least change the comparison: sound and light stop where the wall is, and a guard beacon turns the wall's few decibels into a gain on both sides of the comparison instead of a margin on one side. The non-obvious design move is to **pair two leaky layers whose leaks do not overlap**: a guard beacon where sound escapes (the doorway), sound where radio escapes (the walls). That gets close to the "certainty" hospital systems advertise, using a microphone every phone already has and parts costing a few hundred rupees.

The open question is no longer whether this approach can work in principle; Cisco, Zoom, Microsoft and CourseKey have shipped it. The open question is how the cheapest Indian handsets behave between 18 and 19.5 kHz and how much sound escapes through a propped-open staff-room door. Neither has published data. That gap matters for the pitch: a team that shows per-model acceptance and false-accept rates from its own staff room will have evidence that neither Indian HR suites nor the literature provide. The remaining weakness is an inside accomplice relaying codes, and the system should present that as a deterrence and audit problem (face liveness, random re-checks, anomaly logs, the reverse challenge) rather than claim it is solved.
